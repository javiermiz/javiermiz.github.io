It started on a random night scrolling <a href="https://www.tiktok.com" target="_blank">TikTok</a> before bed. I wasn't looking for anything in particular, just swiping mindlessly like I always do. Then this kid showed up on my feed. He looked 21 or 22. He was showing his work setup, his apartment, his monthly income. Everything about it suggested he was making more than me at 28. And not just that, he spoke with a confidence I didn't even have at 25. The app moved to the next video almost immediately, but the damage was done. **That feeling of being behind, of watching younger people arrive at places you're still walking toward.** I put my phone down and stared at the ceiling for a long time.

This isn't the first time. I've carried this shadow since college. Back then it was friends landing stable jobs while I jumped from one project to another with no certainty. Some were already saving, others bought a car, others posted photos of their new offices. And I celebrated with them, of course, but there was always that knot in my stomach. That little voice saying *they made it, you're still on the road.* It hurts to admit it, but it hurts more to keep it to yourself. Because you tell yourself it goes away with time. That once you get a good job, a decent salary, a title that sounds impressive, the comparison stops.

Then I got the job I have now. Remote, paid in dollars, a salary my 23-year-old self couldn't even dream of. And for a while I felt like I'd won. But comparison doesn't care about your wins. **It doesn't stop when you reach a milestone, it just finds someone new to measure you against.** Now it's not my college friends. It's 19-year-olds on <a href="https://www.youtube.com" target="_blank">YouTube</a> who edit better than me, who have bigger audiences, who make more money from their content. It's creators on TikTok who at 22 already have a loyal following and live off what they make. People I don't even know, living in another country, people I'll probably never meet, but whose success makes me wonder if I'm doing something wrong.

And then there's the other front. The one that doesn't show on a screen but you feel every day. A few months ago I posted a photo on <a href="https://www.instagram.com" target="_blank">Instagram</a> and spent a while scrolling through profiles of younger guys. Guys who are 20, 21, looking jacked, defined, with bodies that look sculpted. And here I am carrying extra weight, with a body that doesn't reflect consistency or discipline. I know what I need to do to change, I've always known. But I struggle to actually do it. Motivation comes in bursts and leaves just as fast, and between one burst and the next, weeks go by without progress. It hurts in a different way. **It's not quite envy, it's more the certainty that there's something I should be doing that I'm not doing.** Seeing their bodies reminds me that even physically, younger people are ahead, reaching a version of themselves I haven't reached yet.

The strangest part is that things are going well. Objectively well. I have a job many would want, a wife who supports me, a roof over my head, my health. On paper I have no reason to complain. But comparison doesn't read your resume, doesn't check your situation before it attacks. It shows up when you least expect it, in the middle of an ordinary night, scrolling TikTok. It makes you forget everything you've built and focus on what others have that you don't. And that makes me think maybe there's no finish line where comparison disappears. **Maybe it's always going to be there, you just change who you compare yourself to.**

I'm not writing this because I found the answer. I'm not going to say I stopped comparing myself or that I learned to celebrate other people's wins without that sting. I'd be lying. I still watch those videos, I still see those bodies, I still feel like I'm falling behind. But at least now I acknowledge it. I sit with it for a moment: *this is happening, this hurts, and it's okay to say it out loud.* Maybe that's the first step I hadn't taken. Not to fix it, but to stop pretending it's not there.

I've started to think comparison isn't something you overcome. It's something you learn to live with. Like background noise you can't fully turn off, but you can turn down eventually. I don't know if I'll still feel this at 30. Probably yes. But at least today, writing it out, it weighs a little less.
